# Anti-pattern catalogue

Use this catalogue for standard or strong editing and audits. These patterns are evidence of weak writing only when they function as filler, vague praise or repeated rhetoric. Preserve precise terms, quotations, titles and deliberate creative choices.

## Filler and vague praise

Avoid: leverage as a verb, utilise, harness, foster, facilitate, streamline, empower, elevate, unlock, unleash, supercharge, revolutionise, delve, metaphorical navigate, underscore as a verb, seamless, robust outside engineering, comprehensive as filler, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, best-in-class, groundbreaking, game-changing, transformative, multifaceted, unprecedented and ever-evolving.

Words such as crucial, pivotal, genuinely, extraordinary, holistic and nuanced can stay when they carry a precise meaning supported by the source. Cut them when they only add emphasis.

Do not replace a weak word with its fashionable synonym. `Capitalise on` for `leverage` and `frictionless` for `seamless` fail the same test. Rewrite the claim in plain terms.

## Stock metaphors and frames

Avoid metaphorical landscape, non-biological ecosystem, realm, paradigm, metaphorical journey or embark, tapestry, testament to, double-edged sword, win-win, synergy, `the world of X`, deep dive, actionable insights and key takeaways.

## Openers and closers

Avoid ceremonial transitions such as moreover, furthermore, additionally, notably, importantly, essentially, in essence and at its core when they add no meaning.

Cut:

- `In conclusion` and `To summarise` when the ending already makes the point.
- `It's worth noting` and `It's important to note`.
- Scene-setting such as `In today's fast-paced world`, `in an era of` and `in the age of`.
- Email furniture such as `I hope this finds you well`, `I wanted to reach out` and `feel free to reach out`.
- Announcement theatre such as `Excited to announce`, `Thrilled to share` and `Delighted to`.
- Empty apologies such as `Apologies for any inconvenience`; say what happened and what changes next.
- Engagement bait, hashtag blocks and decorative emoji.

## Repeated constructions

- **Rule of three:** One rhetorical triad may be natural. Repeated triads become a template. Requested three-part structures and reference data do not count.
- **Manufactured contrast:** Avoid `This is not X, it's Y`, `less about X and more about Y`, and similar templates unless the distinction is genuinely the clearest form.
- **Question and answer:** Avoid `The result? More sales.` and other rhetorical question plus snappy answer patterns in operational prose.
- **Decorative lead-ins:** Avoid bullets such as `**Speed.** We ship daily.` when the bold label adds theatre rather than navigation.
- **Neat bow:** Do not end with an empty uplifting line. End on the action, decision or fact.
- **Hedging stack:** Replace `could potentially`, `may possibly` and similar stacks with the accurate level of uncertainty.
- **Token counterpoint:** Do not append a concession merely to soften a position. Keep counterpoints that affect a decision or action.
- **Metronome prose:** Vary sentence openings, paragraph lengths and endings. Do not manufacture variation when a short uniform format is requested.

In an audit, identify the effect on the reader rather than merely naming a banned token.
